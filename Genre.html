Genres of Photography

Portrait Landscape Street Documentary

📸 Portrait Photography

Capturing the soul behind the face. Portrait photography is one of the oldest and most profound genres. It seeks to reveal the character, mood, and inner life of a person. In our gallery, you can see the work of Julia Margaret Cameron, whose soft-focus, intimate portraits of Victorian intellectuals redefined the genre, and Nadar, who captured the bohemian elite of 19th-century Paris.

Modern portraitists like Richard Avedon and Irving Penn stripped away distractions, using stark white backgrounds to focus entirely on the human face. In our Gallery, you can see how each photographer brings their own vision to the human form.

Key techniques: Lighting (Rembrandt, butterfly, split), lens choice (85mm is classic for portraits), and the all-important connection between photographer and subject.

🌄 Landscape Photography

Where nature meets the sublime. Landscape photography captures the grandeur of the natural world — from majestic mountains to industrial ruins. Our collection features Andreas Gursky, who uses high vantage points to create epic, almost abstract views of modern landscapes, and Edward Burtynsky, who documents the scars of industrialization on the earth.

This genre challenges photographers to find order in chaos, beauty in decay, and to convey the scale of nature. Techniques like long exposure (for silky water) and depth of field (keeping foreground to horizon sharp) are essential tools.

🚶 Street Photography

The poetry of everyday life. Street photography is the art of the unplanned moment. It captures the humor, irony, and humanity of public spaces. Henri Cartier-Bresson, the father of street photography, called it "the decisive moment" — capturing a split second where form, light, and meaning converge.

In our gallery, you'll also find Robert Doisneau (the master of Parisian romance) and Elliott Erwitt (who finds absurdity in the mundane). Street photography relies on speed, intuition, and an intimate knowledge of the 35mm format (as seen in the Technique section).

🌍 Documentary & Photojournalism

Photography as witness. Documentary photography tells stories about real people and social issues. It has the power to change the world. Dorothea Lange's "Migrant Mother" became the face of the Great Depression. Richard Drew's "The Falling Man" is a haunting reminder of 9/11.

Magnum Photos, co-founded by Robert Capa and Henri Cartier-Bresson, became the gold standard for photojournalism. Our Persons page features many Magnum legends: Eve Arnold (the first female member), Ian Berry (who documented Sharpeville), and Steve McCurry (of "Afghan Girl" fame).

This genre demands ethics, empathy, and courage. It reminds us that a photograph is not just an image — it is evidence.
